3.1 Why Arbitrary Point Systems Fail (The Methodological Trap)
Assigning static arbitrary points (e.g., "4 points for a partner, 2 points for
a branch") violates S. S. Stevens's theory of measurement scales (Stevens, 1946).
Because the interval between "2" and "4" reflects an arbitrary opinion rather
than an equal empirical unit, summing those points produces fake precision and
invites gaming under Campbell's Law (Campbell, 1979) and Strathern's Law
(Strathern, 1997): coordinators chase easy 2-point branches instead of high-
leverage institutional commitments.

3.2 Theoretical Foundation: Public Choice Theory Applied to NGO Partnerships
Public Choice Theory applies economic analysis of rational self-interest and
institutional incentives to non-market, public, and civic decision-making
(Mueller, 2003). Five foundational theorems directly govern why Delhi CSR
officers, museum directors, and municipal officials say yes or no:

1. Interdependence Costs — James M. Buchanan & Gordon Tullock (1962),
   *The Calculus of Consent*:
   Every collective agreement imposes two costs on participants:
   - Decision-Making Costs ($D$): Time, outreach attempts, coordination calls,
     and bureaucratic approvals required to reach agreement.
   - External Costs ($E$): Reputational, political, or operational risk suffered
     if the partner aligns with an unreliable initiative.
   Rational cooperation minimizes total interdependence cost ($D + E$). An NGO
   maximizes partnership utility not merely by accumulating raw ties, but by
   generating high verified institutional output per unit of decision-making
   cost ($D$).

2. Selective Incentives & Collective Action — Mancur Olson (1965),
   *The Logic of Collective Action*:
   Public goods (such as "youth governance in Delhi") trigger rational free-
   riding. External institutions rarely commit resources purely for altruism.
   Contribution occurs when the project provides **Selective Incentives**—
   excludable institutional benefits accruing directly to that specific partner
   (e.g., CSR Schedule VII compliance and ESG credit for Agrocell/XYZ; public
   footfall and cultural mandate fulfillment for the City Museum; departmental
   KPI visibility for the Youth Development Office). Actors whose private stake
   justifies bearing upfront fixed costs alone form a "privileged group" (the
   museum providing the exhibition hall).

3. Bureaucratic Utility Maximization — William A. Niskanen (1971),
   *Bureaucracy and Representative Government*:
   Government officials maximize institutional jurisdiction, visible mandate
   delivery, discretionary budget, and career safety (avoiding audit or
   political blame). Ignoring 49 cold NGO invitations is rational bureaucratic
   behavior when external risk ($E$) is unknown and selective reward is zero.

4. Rational Ignorance & Screening Cost Reduction — Anthony Downs (1957) &
   Damon Centola & Michael Macy (2007):
   Evaluating an unknown NGO imposes high information/screening costs. When
   independent trusted institutions converge on a target (Double-, Triple-, or
   Quad-Thread yellow referrals), social proof slashes the target's screening
   and reputational cost ($E$), tipping its private utility equation from
   negative (ignore) to positive (commit).

5. Credible Commitments & Polycentric Durability — Elinor Ostrom (1990),
   *Governing the Commons*:
   Cheap verbal enthusiasm ("we will try to come") carries near-zero utility.
   Utility is revealed only through costly, verifiable commitments (written
   confirmation, shared logo, assigned staff, mobilized network) and becomes
   durable only when borrowed third-party introductions (yellow) convert into
   reciprocal, NGO-owned direct relationships (white).

3.4 Complete Mathematical Specification of the Public Choice Utility Engine
All calculations live in `src/engine/utility.ts` (pure TypeScript, ratio-scale).

--- LAYER A: FUNDING METRIC (KEPT COMPLETELY UNCHANGED AS REQUESTED) ---
Cash funding stays strictly isolated:
$$M_{\text{secured}} = \sum_{e \in E^{\text{tick}}_{\text{green}}} \text{valuePct}(e), \qquad M_{\text{pending}} = \sum_{e \in E^{\text{pending}}_{\text{green}}} \text{valuePct}(e)$$
$$\text{INR}_{\text{secured}} = \frac{M_{\text{secured}}}{100} \times \text{targetBudgetInr}$$

--- LAYER B: PARTNER-SIDE INCENTIVE & SCREENING MODEL (PER PLACEMENT $i$) ---
For any non-root placement $i$, measure why rational choice predicts a "yes":

1. Revealed Co-Ownership Depth ($O_i \in [0, 1]$):
   $$O_i = \frac{\mathbb{I}(\text{written}) + \mathbb{I}(\text{logo}) + \mathbb{I}(\text{staff}) + \mathbb{I}(\text{network})}{4}$$

2. Social Proof / Screening-Cost Reduction Factor ($S_i \in [0, 1)$):
   Let $k^{\text{in}}_Y(i)$ be the count of incoming ticked yellow branches to $i$:
   $$S_i = 1 - \frac{1}{1 + k^{\text{in}}_Y(i)}$$
   Properties:
   - $k^{\text{in}}_Y = 0$ (cold contact): $S_i = 0.00$
   - $k^{\text{in}}_Y = 1$ (1 referral):   $S_i = 0.50$
   - $k^{\text{in}}_Y = 2$ (Double-Thread): $S_i = 0.67$
   - $k^{\text{in}}_Y = 3$ (Triple-Thread): $S_i = 0.75$
   - $k^{\text{in}}_Y = 4$ (Quad-Thread):   $S_i = 0.80$
   This exhibits diminishing marginal utility of additional endorsements while
   quantifying exactly how multi-thread referrals reduce Niskanen/Downs
   screening risk.

3. Incentive Alignment Flag ($I_i \in \{0, 1\}$):
   $$I_i = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } |\text{selectiveIncentives}_i| \ge 1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
   If a `pending` partner has $I_i = 0$ and $S_i = 0$, the software flags a
   **Public Choice Apathy Warning** ("No articulated selective incentive and no
   institutional referral; high probability of rational non-response").

--- LAYER C: NGO-SIDE PARTNER STRUCTURAL UTILITY (PER PLACEMENT $i$) ---
Instead of arbitrary point values, every non-root placement $i$ earns four
strictly bounded, ratio-scale structural utility sub-scores in $[0, 1]$:

Let $N_{\text{conf}}$ be the count of unique non-root placements in the active
Workbook with at least one incoming confirmed (`tick`) branch of any color.
Let $T_{\text{multi}}$ be the count of non-root placements with
$k^{\text{in}}_Y(v) \ge 2$.

1. Direct Commitment Component ($R_i \in [0, 1]$):
   Let $w_i = 1$ if there is a ticked white branch from Root NGO to $i$, else $0$.
   $$R_i = w_i \cdot (0.50 + 0.50 \cdot O_i)$$
   (A confirmed direct white relationship establishes baseline $0.50$; verifiable
   co-ownership behaviors—written commitment, logo, staff, network—raise it
   proportionally up to $1.00$.)

2. Ecosystem Brokerage / Amplification Component ($A_i \in [0, 1]$):
   Let $D^{\text{reach}}_Y(i)$ be the count of unique non-root placements
   reachable strictly downstream from $i$ via directed paths of ticked yellow
   branches (BFS).
   $$A_i = \frac{D^{\text{reach}}_Y(i)}{\max(1, N_{\text{conf}})}$$
   (Represents the exact fraction of the confirmed institutional ecosystem
   unlocked by partner $i$.)

3. Owned Conversion Contribution ($V_i \in [0, 1]$):
   Let $\text{Out}_Y(i)$ be the set of placements receiving a direct ticked
   yellow branch from $i$, and $\text{Owned}(\text{Out}_Y(i))$ the subset that
   also hold a direct ticked white branch from Root NGO:
   $$V_i = \begin{cases} \dfrac{|\text{Owned}(\text{Out}_Y(i))|}{|\text{Out}_Y(i)|} & \text{if } |\text{Out}_Y(i)| > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
   (Rewards partners whose referrals convert into durable NGO-owned ties under
   Ostrom's institutional durability principle.)

4. Multi-Thread Reinforcement Contribution ($C_i \in [0, 1]$):
   Let $m_i$ be the count of targets $v$ with $k^{\text{in}}_Y(v) \ge 2$ to which
   partner $i$ supplies a confirmed ticked yellow branch:
   $$C_i = \begin{cases} \dfrac{m_i}{T_{\text{multi}}} & \text{if } T_{\text{multi}} > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

5. Partner Structural Utility Score ($U_i \in [0, 100]$):
   Weighted linear utility function using transparent, workbook-level policy
   weights $(\alpha_R, \alpha_A, \alpha_V, \alpha_C)$ where
   $\alpha_R + \alpha_A + \alpha_V + \alpha_C = 1.00$:
   $$U_i = 100 \times (\alpha_R R_i + \alpha_A A_i + \alpha_V V_i + \alpha_C C_i)$$

   Default Balanced Strategy Weights (editable per Workbook in Dashboard):
   - $\alpha_R = 0.30$ (Direct Commitment & Co-Ownership)
   - $\alpha_A = 0.35$ (Ecosystem Amplification / Brokerage)
   - $\alpha_V = 0.20$ (Owned Relationship Conversion)
   - $\alpha_C = 0.15$ (Multi-Thread Screening-Cost Reduction)

   Pre-configured Strategy Presets (One-click in Maximized Dashboard):
   - *Balanced Coalition* : $(0.30, 0.35, 0.20, 0.15)$
   - *Amplification Push* : $(0.20, 0.45, 0.15, 0.20)$ (early network growth)
   - *Delivery & Venues*  : $(0.50, 0.20, 0.20, 0.10)$ (execution-heavy events)

   CRITICAL ANTI-BLACK-BOX RULE:
   Whenever $U_i$ is shown in the UI, hovering or expanding MUST display its
   exact 4-part additive decomposition ($100\alpha_R R_i + 100\alpha_A A_i +
   100\alpha_V V_i + 100\alpha_C C_i$) so every single unit is 100% auditable.

6. Decision-Cost Adjusted Utility Yield ($\eta_i$ — Buchanan-Tullock Efficiency):
   Let $d_i = \max(1, \text{outreachAttempts}_i)$:
   $$\eta_i = \frac{U_i}{d_i}$$
   Interpretation: Structural utility units generated per outreach touch.
   Exposes high-leverage partners (e.g., City Museum: $U = 74$ in 3 touches ->
   $\eta = 24.7$/touch) versus high-friction sinks (e.g., an agency consuming 9
   meetings for minimal verified action).

--- LAYER D: WORKBOOK-LEVEL PARTNERSHIP UTILITY INDEX ($\text{PUI} \in [0, 100]$) ---
To summarize the entire Workbook's qualitative progress on the top ribbon and
Landing Page without summing unbounded raw counts, compute four macro ratios:

1. Direct Confirmation & Co-Ownership Realization ($\bar{R} \in [0, 1]$):
   Mean of $(0.50 + 0.50 O_i)$ across all resolved direct white outreach targets
   from Root NGO (declined white targets contribute $0$).
2. Owned Conversion Rate ($\bar{V} \in [0, 1]$):
   $$\bar{V} = \frac{\text{Owned Conversions}}{\max(1, \text{Total Referred Nodes})}$$
3. Multi-Thread Depth Coverage ($\bar{C} \in [0, 1]$):
   $$\bar{C} = \frac{\text{Nodes with } k^{\text{in}}_Y \ge 2}{\max(1, \text{Total Referred Nodes})}$$
4. Incentive Alignment Coverage ($\bar{I} \in [0, 1]$):
   Fraction of all active (`tick` + `pending`) non-root partners with at least
   one articulated `selectiveIncentive` OR $k^{\text{in}}_Y \ge 1$.

Workbook Partnership Utility Index:
$$\text{PUI} = 100 \times (0.35 \bar{R} + 0.25 \bar{V} + 0.20 \bar{C} + 0.20 \bar{I})$$

Paired always with **Workbook Decision-Cost Efficiency**:
$$\text{Confirmed Actions per Touch} = \frac{\text{Total Ticked Branches}}{\sum_i \max(1, d_i)}$$
and **In-Kind Asset Equivalent**:
$$\text{In-Kind } M_{\text{eq}} = \frac{\sum_{e \in E^{\text{tick}}_{\text{white}}} \text{inKindValueInr}(e)}{\text{targetBudgetInr}} \times 100\%$$

SECTION 4: PILLAR 3 — TWO-LAYER RISK ENGINE (ZERO MACHINE LEARNING)
================================================================================
Machine learning is intentionally excluded because small local NGO workspaces
(5 to 30 projects) cannot supply statistically valid labeled training corpora.
Instead, risk management operates across two complementary, auditable layers:

4.1 Layer 1: Deterministic Structural Risk Engine (`src/engine/risk.ts`)
Pure TypeScript, recomputed live on every edit with zero external calls.
Evaluates six explicit parameters and returns exact offending `placementIds`
and `branchIds` so clicking any risk row highlights the culprit on the canvas:

  Parameter R1: Single-Bridge Concentration Risk (Freeman Articulation Loss)
  - Formula : Percentage of confirmed indirect downstream nodes (distance >= 2
              via ticked yellow paths from Root NGO) that become unreachable if
              the single highest-reach non-root bridge node is removed.
  - Thresholds : Low < 35% | Moderate 35-50% | Elevated 50-70% | Critical > 70%

  Parameter R2: Unowned Dependency Exposure (Ostrom Durability Risk)
  - Formula : Percentage of confirmed yellow-referred nodes that still lack a
              direct ticked white branch from Root NGO.
  - Thresholds : Low < 30% | Moderate 30-50% | Elevated 50-75% | Critical > 75%

  Parameter R3: Funding Concentration & Runway Gap
  - Formula : (a) Top single CSR share of total secured `M%`, and
              (b) Remaining gap $(100\% - M_{\text{secured}} - M_{\text{pending}})$.
  - Thresholds : Critical if single donor > 65% of secured cash OR combined
                 secured+pending < 50% of target; Elevated if single donor > 50%.

  Parameter R4: Public Choice Apathy Risk (Olson / Niskanen Cold-Pitch Risk)
  - Formula : Percentage of `pending` non-root nodes having BOTH
              `yellowInDegree === 0` AND `selectiveIncentives.length === 0`.
  - Thresholds : Low < 20% | Moderate 20-40% | Elevated 40-60% | Critical > 60%

  Parameter R5: Decision-Cost Drag & Stale Pipeline
  - Formula : Count of `pending` branches unresolved for > 14 days (from
              `createdAt`) PLUS count of partners with `outreachAttempts >= 4`
              and zero confirmed ticks.
  - Thresholds : Low = 0 | Moderate = 1-2 | Elevated = 3-5 | Critical >= 6

  Parameter R6: Rejection Cluster Signal
  - Formula : Overall resolved rejection ratio `cross / (tick + cross)` OR any
              single partner/category with >= 3 declines and > 60% failure rate.
  - Thresholds : Low < 25% | Moderate 25-45% | Elevated 45-65% | Critical > 65%

  Aggregate Structural Risk Tier:
  - `critical` : Any parameter Critical, OR >= 3 parameters Elevated
  - `elevated` : 1-2 parameters Elevated, OR >= 3 parameters Moderate
  - `moderate` : 1-2 parameters Moderate
  - `low`      : All parameters Low

4.2 Layer 2: Agentic Risk Assessment Box (via MCP + `two-layer-risk-auditor`)
Layer 1 spots mathematical fragility; Layer 2 reads your qualitative branch
notes, partner categories, selective incentives, and real-world Indian
civic/CSR context (e.g., election model code of conduct delays, municipal
officer transfers, CSR fiscal year-end Q4 lockups) and writes a structured
briefing directly into `workbook.riskAssessment`.

The **Risk Assessment Box** appears as a compact status chip on the canvas top
ribbon and expands in full inside the **Maximized Dashboard**, displaying:

6.1 Why Portable Workspace Folders Replace Single-File SQLite
Single-file SQLite databases use companion `-wal` / `-journal` files that risk
database corruption if synced concurrently across Windows, Linux, macOS,
Android, and Google Drive (SQLite, 2026). Plain JSON files inside a structured
Workspace Folder sync cleanly over Google Drive, work identically across every
OS, remain human-readable, and let local MCP servers read/write directly
without native database driver mismatches.

8.1 Concurrency, Atomicity & Live Reload Protocol
1. Atomic Writes: Every MCP mutation writes to `<file>.tmp` and performs an
   atomic `fs.rename()` to prevent half-written JSON files.
2. Pre-Write Undo Snapshots: Before any MCP write alters a workbook, copy the
   previous JSON into `<Workspace>/.snapshots/<wbId>_<timestamp>.json` (keep
   last 20 snapshots per workbook) so the user can click `Undo Agent Change`
   in the UI.
3. Revision Increment & Audit Trail: Every MCP write increments `revision`,
   updates `updatedAt`, and appends a concise line to `lastAgentMutation`
   (`{ agentName, toolName, summary, timestamp }`).
4. Live UI Refresh: The Tauri Rust backend (or Vite dev bridge in Phase 2A)
   watches the active Workspace Folder via OS file watcher (`notify` crate),
   debounces 150ms, validates the changed JSON, and pushes a live state patch
   to Zustand so the canvas and Maximized Dashboard update automatically.

# Partnership Graph Operator Skill

## Core Domain Rules (NEVER VIOLATE)
1. **Always Search Before Creating**: Before calling `add_or_import_entity`, ALWAYS call `search_entities` with partial keywords (e.g., "collectorate", "museum", "agrocell") to reuse existing master entities and avoid duplicates.
2. **Strict Three-Color Branch Semantics**:
   - `green`: Cash CSR/grant funding ONLY. `valuePct` MUST be a strict percentage of `targetBudgetInr` (e.g., ₹2.5L on ₹10L target = `25`, displayed as `M25`). Never put non-cash items on green lines.
   - `white`: Direct working relationships owned by the Root NGO (venues, attendance, co-hosting, direct staff ties). Optional verifiable `inKindValueInr` allowed for real market-value venue/asset savings.
   - `yellow`: Third-party introductions, referrals, and amplification bridges.
3. **Ownership Separation Rule**:
   - If Partner A introduces the NGO to Agency B, create a `yellow` branch `A -> B`.
   - NEVER draw a `white` branch `A -> B` to represent the NGO's relationship with B.
   - Only when Agency B directly engages with the Root NGO should you add a `white` branch `Root NGO -> B`.
4. **Preserve Declined Memory**:
   - When an organization says no, call `update_branch` with `status: "cross"` instead of `delete_branch`. Only delete genuine typos or accidental duplicates.
5. **No False Confirmations**:
   - Planned outreach or unconfirmed introductions MUST start as `status: "pending"`. Only set `status: "tick"` when concrete verification exists in user notes.
6. **Keep the Canvas Clean**:
   - After adding 2 or more nodes or branches, ALWAYS call `run_tidy_up_layout(workbookId)` so the graph stays organized bottom-to-top with zero overlapping cards.

## Standard Workflow
1. Call `list_workbooks` or `get_workbook_full(workbookId)` to inspect current state.
2. Deduplicate entities via `search_entities`.
3. Execute targeted mutations (`add_or_import_entity`, `create_branch`, `update_branch`, `update_placement_attributes`).
4. Run `run_tidy_up_layout(workbookId)`.
5. Summarize changes concisely: nodes added/reused, branches updated, new `M%` funding, and any newly formed Double/Triple-Thread convergences.

# Public Choice Utility Analyst Skill

## Analytical Mandate
You diagnose why CSR funders, public museums, and municipal agencies commit or ignore outreach, and you keep the Workbook's quantitative utility inputs strictly grounded in verifiable evidence.

## Strict Prohibition
NEVER invent subjective 1-to-5 trust ratings or arbitrary static point scores. Every number MUST come from binary co-ownership facts, articulated selective incentives, verified graph topology, and integer outreach attempt counts.

## Step-by-Step Protocol
1. Call `get_workbook_full(workbookId)` and `get_public_choice_utility_report(workbookId)`.
2. Inspect every non-root placement for **Olson / Niskanen Incentive Blindness**:
   - Flag any pending target with `selectiveIncentives: []` and `yellowInDegree === 0`.
   - Based on the entity's category and branch notes, propose concrete selective incentives (e.g., `csr_compliance`, `institutional_mandate`, `brand_visibility`, `network_prestige`, `asset_activation`) and ask or update via `update_placement_attributes`.
3. Audit **Ostrom Credible Commitments (`coOwnershipSignals`)**:
   - Review notes for concrete evidence of `writtenCommitment`, `brandOrLogoShared`, `staffAssigned`, and `networkMobilized`. Only toggle a boolean to `true` when explicitly supported by project notes or user confirmation.
4. Audit **Buchanan-Tullock Decision-Cost Efficiency ($\eta_i$)**:
   - Identify partners with high `outreachAttempts` and low Utility Yield ($\eta_i$). Recommend either shifting the pitch to a clearer selective incentive, routing a second yellow referral to raise Social Proof $S_i$, or marking the branch `cross` to stop burning team hours.
5. Audit **Ostrom Durability (Borrowed vs. Owned Ties)**:
   - List confirmed yellow-referred offices lacking a direct `white` branch from the Root NGO, and recommend concrete actions to convert borrowed introductions into owned relationships.

# Two-Layer Risk Auditor Skill

## Purpose
Replace ungrounded guesswork or unviable ML models with a rigorous, repeatable risk briefing persisted directly inside the app's Maximized Dashboard Risk Assessment Box.

## Audit Protocol (6 Mandatory Parameters)
1. Call `get_workbook_full(workbookId)`, `get_public_choice_utility_report(workbookId)`, and `get_structural_risk_report(workbookId)`.
2. Review all six Layer 1 structural outputs:
   - `R1`: Single-Bridge Concentration Risk (articulation node failure impact)
   - `R2`: Unowned Dependency Exposure (yellow-only referrals without NGO white tie)
   - `R3`: Funding Concentration & Budget Runway Gap (`M%` concentration + shortfall)
   - `R4`: Public Choice Apathy Risk (cold pending targets lacking incentives & referrals)
   - `R5`: Decision-Cost Drag & Stale Pipeline (pending > 14 days or >= 4 touches unresolved)
   - `R6`: Rejection Cluster Signal (high decline rates by category or parent)
3. Synthesize Layer 1 numbers with Layer 2 qualitative context from branch notes (e.g., government officer transfer risk, CSR budget cycle timing, verbal-only venue holds without written confirmation, single-contact dependency).
4. Draft 3 to 6 concrete, non-generic `failureScenarios`. Every scenario MUST specify:
   - Clear `title`, `severity`, and `likelihood`
   - Exact `affectedPlacementIds` (verified against current workbook IDs)
   - Specific `triggerCondition` (what exact real-world event triggers failure)
   - Concrete, actionable `mitigationAction` doable within 7-14 days
5. Write a sharp `publicChoiceDiagnosis` explaining where partner self-interest is unaligned or where a 2nd/3rd yellow referral would unlock a stalled target.
6. Call `write_risk_assessment_box(...)` to publish the audit live to the dashboard.

# Spreadsheet-to-Workflow Mapper Skill

## Purpose
Real NGO spreadsheets contain messy column headers, Indian currency strings ("2.5L", "Rs 50,000", "1 Cr"), cold outreach lists of 100+ rows, and free-text status notes. Convert them into a clean, readable partnership graph without flooding the canvas.

## Strict Ingestion Rules
1. **Read Context First**: Call `get_workbook_full(workbookId)` and `search_entities("")` to learn the Workbook's `targetBudgetInr`, Root NGO placement ID, and existing entity names.
2. **Prevent the 100-Node Canvas Tangle (Ghoniem et al., 2005)**:
   - If the spreadsheet has > 30 rows, DO NOT blindly dump 100 cold uncontacted rows onto the visual canvas!
   - Separate rows into: (a) Active / Confirmed / Referred / Explicitly Declined rows -> import onto the canvas; (b) Uncontacted cold prospects -> ask the user whether to import only the top priority tier (10-25 nodes) or group them cleanly.
3. **Currency Normalization (India-Aware)**:
   - Convert rupee strings (`₹2.5 Lakh` = 250000, `₹1.2 Cr` = 12000000, `50k` = 50000) into strict `valuePct = (amountInr / targetBudgetInr) * 100` rounded to 1 decimal place for `green` branches, or `inKindValueInr` for non-cash `white` assets.
4. **Status Mapping**:
   - Positive confirmed commitments -> `"tick"`
   - Declined / rejected / no budget -> `"cross"`
   - Contacted / awaiting reply / follow-up -> `"pending"`
5. **Referral Chain Detection**:
   - If a row has a `"Referred By"` or `"Introduced Through"` column naming Organization A, generate:
     (1) `yellow` branch `A -> Target` (`tick` if intro happened, `pending` if requested), AND
     (2) `white` branch `Root NGO -> Target` ONLY if the Target directly replied/engaged with the NGO.
6. **Commit & Tidy**:
   - Call `batch_import_workflow_rows(workbookId, rows, runTidyUpAfter: true)`.
   - Report exact counts: imported, deduplicated/reused, skipped cold rows, and any ambiguous rows needing user review.

SECTION 10: MASTER TYPESCRIPT DATA SCHEMA (VERSION 2 — src/types/schema.ts)
================================================================================
All files remain 100% JSON-serializable (no Maps, Sets, or class instances) so
TypeScript, Tauri Rust `serde`, Google Drive JSON files, and the MCP server
share the exact same contract.

  export type WorkspaceSourceType = 'local' | 'gdrive';

  export type EntityCategory =
    | 'ngo'
    | 'csr'
    | 'government'
    | 'institution'
    | 'community'
    | 'other';

  export type BranchType = 'green' | 'white' | 'yellow';
  export type BranchStatus = 'pending' | 'tick' | 'cross';

  export type SelectiveIncentiveTag =
    | 'csr_compliance'
    | 'brand_visibility'
    | 'institutional_mandate'
    | 'policy_influence'
    | 'network_prestige'
    | 'asset_activation'
    | 'community_legitimacy';

  export interface CoOwnershipChecklist {
    writtenCommitment: boolean;
    brandOrLogoShared: boolean;
    staffAssigned: boolean;
    networkMobilized: boolean;
  }

  export interface UtilityStrategyWeights {
    presetName: 'balanced' | 'amplification' | 'delivery' | 'custom';
    alphaR: number; // Direct commitment & co-ownership
    alphaA: number; // Ecosystem brokerage / amplification
    alphaV: number; // Owned relationship conversion
    alphaC: number; // Multi-thread reinforcement
  }

  export interface EntityOriginReference {
    workspaceName: string;
    workbookTitle: string;
    originalEntityId: string;
    importedAt: string;
  }

  // Stored in <Workspace>/entities.json
  export interface Entity {
    entityId: string;
    name: string;
    category: EntityCategory;
    globalNotes: string;
    importedFrom?: EntityOriginReference; // Present when copied across workspaces
    createdAt: string;
    updatedAt: string;
  }

  export interface EntitiesFile {
    schemaVersion: 2;
    revision: number;
    updatedAt: string;
    entities: Record<string, Entity>;
  }

  // Stored inside <Workspace>/workbooks/wb_<id>.json
  export interface Placement {
    placementId: string;
    entityId: string;
    isRootNgo: boolean;
    x: number;
    y: number;
    width: number;
    height: number;
    // Version 2 Public Choice Theory attributes:
    selectiveIncentives: SelectiveIncentiveTag[];
    coOwnershipSignals: CoOwnershipChecklist;
    outreachAttempts: number; // Integer >= 1 (Buchanan-Tullock decision cost)
  }

  export interface Branch {
    branchId: string;
    sourcePlacementId: string;
    targetPlacementId: string;
    type: BranchType;
    status: BranchStatus;
    valuePct?: number;       // Green only: % of targetBudgetInr
    inKindValueInr?: number; // White only: optional verifiable asset value
    note: string;
    createdAt: string;
    updatedAt: string;
    resolvedAt?: string;
  }

  export type RiskSeverity = 'low' | 'moderate' | 'elevated' | 'critical';

  export interface RiskFailureScenario {
    id: string;
    title: string;
    severity: RiskSeverity;
    likelihood: 'low' | 'medium' | 'high';
    triggerCondition: string;
    affectedPlacementIds: string[];
    mitigationAction: string;
    status: 'open' | 'mitigating' | 'resolved';
  }

  export interface AgentRiskAssessment {
    assessedAt: string;
    assessedBy: string; // e.g., "Claude Code via MCP" or "Human"
    overallPosition: string;
    parameterCommentary: Record<'R1' | 'R2' | 'R3' | 'R4' | 'R5' | 'R6', string>;
    failureScenarios: RiskFailureScenario[];
    publicChoiceDiagnosis: string;
  }

  export interface AgentMutationStamp {
    agentName: string;
    toolName: string;
    summary: string;
    timestamp: string;
  }

  // Stored in <Workspace>/workbooks/wb_<id>.json
  export interface WorkbookFile {
    schemaVersion: 2;
    workbookId: string;
    revision: number;
    title: string;
    description: string;
    targetBudgetInr: number;
    status: 'active' | 'draft' | 'archived';
    utilityWeights: UtilityStrategyWeights;
    placements: Record<string, Placement>;
    branches: Record<string, Branch>;
    riskAssessment?: AgentRiskAssessment;
    lastAgentMutation?: AgentMutationStamp;
    createdAt: string;
    updatedAt: string;
  }

  export interface WorkbookSummaryRef {
    workbookId: string;
    fileName: string; // e.g., "workbooks/wb_youth_exhibition.json"
    title: string;
    status: 'active' | 'draft' | 'archived';
    updatedAt: string;
  }

  export interface WorkbookLink {
    linkId: string;
    sourceWorkbookId: string;
    targetWorkbookId: string;
    relationLabel: string;
    note: string;
  }

  // Stored in <Workspace>/workspace.json
  export interface WorkspaceManifest {
    schemaVersion: 2;
    workspaceId: string;
    name: string;
    revision: number;
    workbooks: Record<string, WorkbookSummaryRef>;
    workbookLinks: Record<string, WorkbookLink>;
    createdAt: string;
    updatedAt: string;
  }

  // Device-local read-only index for cross-workspace entity search
  export interface RecentEntityIndexEntry {
    entityId: string;
    name: string;
    normalizedName: string;
    category: EntityCategory;
    workspaceId: string;
    workspaceName: string;
    sourceType: WorkspaceSourceType;
    workbookMentions: Array<{
      workbookId: string;
      workbookTitle: string;
      confirmedWhiteWithNgo: boolean;
      confirmedYellowOutCount: number;
      declinedCount: number;
    }>;
  }
